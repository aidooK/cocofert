Why we exist

Meet Madam Yaa.

Madam Yaa runs a small shop in one of Navrongo's coconut-selling communities, surrounded by traders who shell coconuts every day. With no formal system to deal with the waste, the discarded husks pile up outside her shop faster than anyone can clear them.

"We used to burn them or dump them in the gutter. There was nowhere else for them to go."

— A shop owner in one of Ghana's coconut-selling communities
The problem

Husks aren't harmless.

  • They clog drains. Left in the street, discarded husks block gutters and fuel seasonal flooding.
  • They spread disease. Rotting piles pollute communities and become breeding grounds for pests.
  • They're burned instead. Communities set husks alight, trading one pollution problem for another.
The solution

The same husks, rebuilt into fertility.

CocoFert collects the coconut husk waste already piling up in local communities and turns it into a 25kg bag of 100% organic fertilizer — nothing wasted, nothing synthetic.

1. Collection

We partner directly with coconut sellers to collect husks that would otherwise be burned or dumped.

2. Shredding

Husks are mechanically broken down into fine, uniform organic matter ready for composting.

3. Composting & Enrichment

The shredded husk is composted and enriched into nutrient-dense, soil-ready fertilizer.

4. CocoFert, bagged

Packed into 25kg bags and delivered to farms, agro-shops, and gardens — ready to nourish soil.
